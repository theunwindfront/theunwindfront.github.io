AI Doesn't Always Mean Building a New App

Sagar Pansuriya · September 15, 2026 · 4 min read

When we hear "Artificial Intelligence" in the context of business, our minds often jump to complex, expensive solutions. We envision custom-built mobile apps, intricate databases, and months of software development.

But what if making your business smarter doesn't require building anything new at all?

I recently had an experience that perfectly illustrates this point. My father-in-law runs an agriculture medicine business through a specific franchise, managing an inventory of around 120 different products and their unique selling propositions (USPs).

One day, he came to me with a very practical, real-world problem:

"Can we build a mobile app where I can upload another brand's medicine photos and find the most relevant medicine from our products, along with its benefits and usage?"

The Instinct to Build

Technically speaking? Absolutely, yes. We could build that app.

As a developer, my brain immediately started mapping out the architecture:

  • A custom database to house the 120 products.
  • A search engine with matching logic.
  • An admin panel for updates.
  • A sleek mobile UI for field usage.

But before writing a single line of code, I stopped and asked myself a crucial question: Do we really need to build all of that?

The Practical Solution

Instead of embarking on a months-long software development journey, I gave him NotebookLM.

I gathered all the relevant product information, technical specs, and USPs, and organized them into a knowledge base that he could interact with using AI.

The transformation was immediate. Now, instead of manually sifting through 120 products or relying purely on memory, he simply asks questions. He can compare information, find relevant products to match competitors, and get clear explanations that help him communicate benefits and usage criteria to his customers.

The Real AI Opportunity

The most interesting part of this story isn't the technology itself. It's the fact that my father-in-law isn't a software developer. He runs a traditional, boots-on-the-ground business. Yet, he adopted AI seamlessly because it solved a very real, tangible problem for him without adding friction.

And that's where I think many businesses are getting AI wrong right now.

AI doesn't always mean building a complicated, AI-powered application from scratch. Often, the formula for success is much simpler:

Your Existing Knowledge + AI = A Much Smarter Workflow

If AI can help a traditional agriculture medicine business drastically improve how it searches, understands, and utilizes its product knowledge, what's stopping your business from trying it?

The opportunity isn't found in asking, "How do I add AI to my business?"

The better, more actionable question is: "What part of my business is still unnecessarily manual?"

That's exactly where I would start.
